Software should follow the work

When software doesn’t fit, the work finds a way around it.

Sometimes the process is reshaped to suit the software. Sometimes the awkward parts move into spreadsheets, email and separate files.

The team keeps the work moving. The information gets left behind: copied entry, detached approvals, competing versions and reports that arrive behind the work.

The problem is the break in connected information and accountability between the tools.

The software follows the work.

I trace how the work really moves: where information begins, who owns the next action, what must be checked and what evidence needs to survive. Then I build the smallest shared layer that keeps it together.

AI changes what’s practical.

For years, off-the-shelf software was often the practical choice: bespoke layers were costly and slow to deliver. AI-assisted development makes earlier working demonstrations and shorter iterations possible. That can make a focused bespoke software layer a more affordable, viable investment. Using AI to build software is separate from deciding whether it should handle your operational data.

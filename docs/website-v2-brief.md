## Positioning and copy

Lead with: **When software doesn’t fit, the work finds a way around it.**

Explain the two common responses in plain language: reshape the process to suit the software, or take parts of the work into spreadsheets, email and separate files. The break is in connected information and accountability: repeated entry, detached approvals, competing versions and reports that arrive behind the work. Do not imply every spreadsheet is bad or all packaged software should be replaced.

Response: **Keep the software that works. Connect the work that falls between it.**

Proof Systems understands the actual workflow, then builds only the missing connections and tools, keeping information, ownership and history attached as work moves forward. Operational systems are the primary offer. AI and automation are selective supporting methods; team training is a supporting route. Use Mat's first-person voice, British English, concrete language and accurate claims. No invented customers, results, endorsements or sector breadth.

## Experience and art direction

Create a carefully composed, modern website with ink, warm cream and restrained amber from the current brand, self-hosted Inter, generous typographic scale, varied spacing and overlapping visual layers. Aim for an authored business website rather than a slide deck or generic SaaS card grid.

The main visual should be a generic, clearly labelled illustrative workflow: one identifiable piece of work travels from request through delivery, approval and accounts. A detached note or spreadsheet demonstrates how context is lost, then a connected record carries the source, owner, status and history together. Show meaningful fragments of information at different depths with a continuous connector or shared reference. Avoid isometric cubes, bevelled tiles, generic icon collections and repetitive arrows between labelled boxes. Do not show construction product screenshots as the main hero.

Use one main explanatory scroll sequence after the opener. Other sections move in ordinary page flow with modest reveal effects; avoid a succession of pinned full-screen chapters. Mobile must be compact, legible and touch-friendly, with a purposeful simplified composition. Essential text and CTAs must be available with reduced motion and without JavaScript; no permanently hidden content or focusable invisible controls. Keep scrolling native.

Suggested narrative: opening B → software-fit problem → continuous illustrative disconnected-to-connected work → practical support and a short approach → restrained evidence → operator experience → discuss a workflow. Combine sections where that improves pace. Navigation should reflect the new story. Preserve existing useful destinations and any legacy homepage anchor routes where practical.
